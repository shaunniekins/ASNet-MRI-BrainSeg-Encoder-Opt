\begin{abstract}
\begin{singlespacing}
    Accurate and efficient segmentation of brain tumors from Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is crucial for diagnosis and treatment planning. Deep learning models, particularly those incorporating attention mechanisms like the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net), have shown promise. However, the original AS-Net, designed for skin lesion segmentation, utilized the computationally heavy VGG16 encoder. This study aimed to optimize AS-Net for MRI brain tumor segmentation by comparing the performance of the VGG16 encoder against lightweight alternatives: MobileNetV3 (Large and Small) and EfficientNetV2 (B0 and B1). The core AS-Net architecture, featuring Spatial Attention (SAM), Channel Attention (CAM), and Synergy modules, was integrated with each encoder (all using a 224x224 input size except ENv2-B1 at 240x240) and evaluated on the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset, which was preprocessed from NIfTI format into 2D HDF5 slices. Performance was assessed based on segmentation accuracy metrics (Dice Coefficient/F1 Score, IoU) and computational efficiency (total training time). Results evaluated on the test set demonstrated that the VGG16 baseline achieved the highest segmentation accuracy (Dice: 0.8873), very closely followed by EfficientNetV2-B0 (Dice: 0.8853) and EfficientNetV2-B1 (Dice: 0.8815). The lightweight models offered substantial efficiency gains in terms of training time compared to VGG16 (e.g., MobileNetV3-Large trained approx. 5.4 hours faster [~23\% reduction], ENv2-B0 approx. 4.7 hours faster [~20\% reduction]). EfficientNetV2-B0 provided the best balance among the lightweight options, achieving accuracy nearly identical to VGG16 with significantly improved computational efficiency. This research concludes that while VGG16 yielded the marginally highest accuracy in this setup, optimizing AS-Net with lightweight encoders like EfficientNetV2-B0 presents a highly viable trade-off for practical MRI brain tumor segmentation, balancing top-tier performance with enhanced efficiency.

\end{singlespacing}    
\end{abstract}
