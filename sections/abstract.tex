\begin{abstract}

Accurate and efficient segmentation of brain tumors from Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is crucial for diagnosis and treatment planning. Deep learning models, particularly those incorporating attention mechanisms like the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net), have shown promise. However, the original AS-Net, designed for skin lesion segmentation, utilized the computationally heavy VGG16 encoder, limiting its practical efficiency. This study aimed to optimize AS-Net for MRI brain tumor segmentation by comparing the performance of the original VGG16 encoder against lightweight alternatives: MobileNetV3 (Large and Small) and EfficientNetV2 (B0 and B1). The core AS-Net architecture, featuring Spatial Attention (SAM), Channel Attention (CAM), and Synergy modules, was integrated with each encoder and evaluated on the BraTS 2020 dataset (preprocessed H5 slices). Performance was assessed based on segmentation accuracy metrics (Dice Coefficient, IoU) and computational efficiency (training time). Results demonstrated that lightweight encoders, specifically MobileNetV3-Large (Dice: 0.9326) and EfficientNetV2-B0 (Dice: 0.9295), achieved segmentation accuracy comparable or slightly superior to the VGG16 baseline (Dice: 0.9319). Crucially, these lightweight models offered substantial efficiency gains, reducing training time by approximately 7-8 times. MobileNetV3-Large provided the best overall balance of high accuracy and significantly improved computational efficiency. This research concludes that optimizing AS-Net with lightweight encoders, particularly MobileNetV3-Large, yields an effective and practical solution for MRI brain tumor segmentation, enhancing its potential for clinical application.

\end{abstract}
