This chapter reviews the existing literature relevant to MRI brain tumor segmentation using deep learning, providing the context for the current study. It begins by discussing established approaches to brain tumor segmentation, covering both traditional image processing techniques and the more recent deep learning methods, while also highlighting persistent challenges in the field. Subsequently, it delves into the concept and application of attention mechanisms within deep learning, particularly their utility in medical image segmentation. \section{Brain Tumor Segmentation}

Approaches for segmenting brain tumors from medical imaging data includes traditional techniques like thresholding, region-growing, active contours and more recent deep learning methods like convolutional neural networks.

\subsection{Traditional Methods}

Traditional methods for brain tumor segmentation have relied on classical image processing techniques, such as thresholding, region-growing, and active contour models. Thresholding methods segment images by setting intensity values above or below a certain threshold to a specific value, effectively separating the tumor region from the surrounding tissues \parencite{gordillo2013state}. Region-growing algorithms, on the other hand, start from a seed point and iteratively expand the region by adding neighboring pixels that satisfy certain similarity criteria \parencite{melouah2018overview}. Active contour models, also known as snakes, deform an initial contour towards the boundaries of the tumor region based on energy minimization principles \parencite{caselles1997geodesic}.

While these traditional methods have been widely used, they often face limitations in handling complex tumor shapes, intensity variations, and image artifacts. For instance, thresholding techniques struggle to differentiate tumors from surrounding tissues with similar intensities, and they are susceptible to noise \parencite{cai2018quantitative}. Region-growing methods can also be sensitive to the initial seed placement and may fail to accurately segment tumors with irregular shapes or heterogeneous intensities \parencite{gordillo2013state}. Active contour models, while capable of handling complex shapes, often require careful parameter tuning and can be computationally intensive, limiting their practical applicability in high-throughput scenarios \parencite{pierre2021segmentation}.

\subsection{Deep Learning Methods}

The advent of deep learning, particularly Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs), has revolutionized the field of medical image analysis, including brain tumor segmentation. DCNNs have demonstrated a remarkable ability to automatically learn hierarchical features and capture complex spatial relationships directly from image data, leading to more accurate and robust segmentation outcomes compared to traditional methods \parencite{litjens2017survey}.

One of the most influential DCNN architectures for biomedical image segmentation is the U-Net \parencite{ronneberger2015u}. Its encoder-decoder structure with skip connections effectively combines deep, semantic features with fine-grained spatial details, making it well-suited for precise localization. Numerous variants and extensions of the U-Net have been proposed. Other prominent architectures employed for segmentation include SegNet \parencite{badrinarayanan2017segnet}, which utilizes pooling indices for efficient upsampling, and DeepLabv3+ \parencite{chen2018encoder}, which incorporates atrous (dilated) convolutions and spatial pyramid pooling to capture multi-scale context.

These deep learning-based methods have consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance on brain tumor segmentation benchmarks, demonstrating their capacity to handle the variability in tumor appearance, shape, and location, as well as image noise \parencite{gupta2021deep, bakas2018identifying}. Frameworks like nnU-Net \parencite{isensee2021nnu}, which automatically configures U-Net architectures based on dataset properties, often define the benchmark performance on challenges such as BraTS, showcasing the power of well-tuned deep learning pipelines. However, their success is often contingent on the availability of large, accurately annotated datasets for training.

\subsection{Challenges in Brain Tumor Segmentation}

Despite the significant progress enabled by deep learning, several challenges persist in the field of automated brain tumor segmentation:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Scarcity and Annotation Cost:} Acquiring large-scale medical imaging datasets with high-quality, voxel-level annotations is often difficult and expensive. Manual segmentation by clinical experts is time-consuming and requires specialized knowledge, limiting the availability of ground truth data for training supervised models \parencite{ullah2023enhancing}.
    \item \textbf{Class Imbalance:} In typical brain MRI scans, tumor regions occupy a much smaller volume compared to the background (healthy tissue). This inherent class imbalance can bias deep learning models towards the majority class, potentially hindering the accurate segmentation of smaller tumor components \parencite{isensee2018brain}. Techniques like specialized loss functions (e.g., Dice loss, Focal loss) or sampling strategies are often needed to mitigate this issue.
    \item \textbf{Tumor Heterogeneity:} Brain tumors exhibit significant variability in terms of shape, size, location, texture, and intensity profiles across different patients and tumor types (e.g., glioma grades, metastases). Models trained on one type of data may not generalize well to unseen variations \parencite{ranjbarzadeh2021brain}. Capturing the diverse manifestations of tumors, including components like enhancing core, necrotic core, and peritumoral edema, remains a challenge \parencite{bakas2018identifying}.
    \item \textbf{Computational Complexity:} Many high-performing deep learning models, especially those using deep architectures or processing 3D volumetric data, are computationally intensive. Training these models requires significant computational resources (GPUs, memory) and time. Inference speed can also be a bottleneck for real-time clinical applications \parencite{havaei2017brain}.
\end{enumerate}

Addressing these challenges, particularly the need for computational efficiency without sacrificing accuracy, is a key motivation for exploring architectures like AS-Net with lightweight backbones.

\section{Attention Mechanisms in Deep Learning}

Attention mechanisms represent a powerful concept in deep learning, enabling models to selectively focus on the most informative parts of the input data and capture long-range dependencies, thereby improving performance on various tasks, including medical image segmentation.

\subsection{Types of Attention Mechanisms}

Attention mechanisms dynamically modulate feature representations based on their relevance to the task. Several types have been influential:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Attention:} These mechanisms learn to assign higher weights to specific spatial locations within an image or feature map that are deemed more important. By highlighting salient regions (e.g., potential tumor areas) and suppressing irrelevant background, spatial attention helps the model focus its representational capacity \parencite{jetley2018learn}.
    \item \textbf{Channel Attention:} These mechanisms focus on the relationships between different feature channels. They learn to adaptively rescale channel-wise features, emphasizing those that are most discriminative for the task and attenuating less useful ones. The Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block is a prominent example \parencite{hu2018squeeze}.
    \item \textbf{Self-Attention:} Originally popularized by the Transformer architecture \parencite{vaswani2017attention}, self-attention allows the model to weigh the influence of different positions within a sequence or feature map on each other. This enables the capture of long-range dependencies and global context, which can be crucial for understanding complex structures in images. Variants of self-attention are increasingly used in vision tasks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Applications in Medical Image Segmentation}

Attention mechanisms have been successfully applied to various medical image segmentation tasks, including brain tumor segmentation. A foundational approach is the Attention U-Net \parencite{oktay2018attention}, which introduced attention gates within the U-Net skip connections. These gates learn to suppress irrelevant feature activations from the encoder before they are merged with decoder features, effectively highlighting salient regions for tasks like pancreas and, subsequently, brain tumor segmentation. Building on such principles, the study by \textcite{sinha2020multi} proposed an attention-based U-Net architecture that incorporates spatial and channel attention modules, demonstrating improved segmentation performance for brain tumors compared to the vanilla U-Net. This study has shown that attention mechanisms can effectively guide deep learning models to focus on the most relevant features and spatial locations, leading to improved segmentation accuracy and robustness.

\section{Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net)}

The Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) is a deep learning architecture specifically designed by \textcite{hu2022net} for image segmentation, initially demonstrated on skin lesion segmentation tasks. Its core novelty lies in the synergistic integration of spatial and channel attention mechanisms within its decoder.

\subsection{Architecture and Methodology}

The AS-Net follows a typical encoder-decoder structure. An encoder backbone (originally VGG16) extracts multi-level feature maps from the input image. The decoder then progressively upsamples these features, integrating information from corresponding encoder levels via skip connections, to produce the final segmentation map. The key innovation resides in the decoder's building blocks:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Attention Module (SAM):} This module processes encoder features to generate spatial attention maps, highlighting regions likely belonging to the target object (e.g., lesion or tumor). It uses parallel convolutional pathways and pooling/upsampling to capture multi-scale spatial context.
    \item \textbf{Channel Attention Module (CAM):} Operating in parallel to SAM, this module computes channel-wise attention weights based on global information, allowing the network to emphasize the most informative feature channels.
    \item \textbf{Synergy Module:} This module adaptively fuses the outputs from the SAM and CAM branches. It learns weighting parameters ($\alpha$ and $\beta$) to combine the spatially-focused and channel-focused feature maps, aiming to leverage the complementary strengths of both attention types. A final convolution refines the fused features.
\end{enumerate}

This combination of parallel attention pathways followed by synergistic fusion is applied at multiple stages of the decoder, allowing the network to refine its focus at different resolutions.

\subsection{Performance and Limitations in Skin Lesion Segmentation}

In its original publication, AS-Net achieved state-of-the-art results on several benchmark datasets for skin lesion segmentation, demonstrating the effectiveness of its attention synergy approach \parencite{hu2022net}. The synergistic attention was shown to outperform models using only spatial or only channel attention, or simpler combination methods.

However, the authors acknowledged limitations inherent in their implementation:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Computational Efficiency:} The reliance on the VGG16 encoder resulted in a model with a large number of parameters and relatively high computational cost (FLOPs). This limits inference speed and increases memory requirements, potentially hindering deployment in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications.
    \item \textbf{Generalizability:} While highly effective for skin lesions, the performance of AS-Net on other medical imaging tasks, such as MRI brain tumor segmentation, was not explored. The optimal configuration and effectiveness might vary depending on the imaging modality and target structures.
\end{enumerate}

The authors explicitly suggested exploring alternative, more lightweight encoder architectures to address the efficiency concerns, which forms the primary motivation for the research presented in this thesis.

\section{Lightweight Encoder Architectures}

Computationally efficient deep learning models are increasingly important in medical image analysis, balancing the need for high accuracy with practical constraints on resources and processing time. Lightweight encoder architectures are specifically designed to achieve this balance.

\subsection{Importance of Lightweight Models in Medical Imaging}

The demand for efficient models in medical imaging stems from several factors:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Real-time Applications:} Clinical workflows often benefit from rapid analysis, such as during image-guided interventions or for quick preliminary assessments. Models with low latency are crucial \parencite{ullah2023enhancing}.
    \item \textbf{Deployment on Diverse Hardware:} There is growing interest in deploying AI tools not just on powerful servers but also on hospital workstations, portable devices, or even edge computing platforms closer to the patient \parencite{hirschorn2014use}. These platforms often have limited computational power and memory.
    \item \textbf{Scalability and Cost:} Training and running large deep learning models can incur significant computational costs (hardware, energy). Efficient models reduce this burden, making AI solutions more accessible and scalable across healthcare systems \parencite{canziani2016analysis}.
    \item \textbf{Faster Research and Development:} Lighter models typically train faster, accelerating the research cycle for developing and iterating on new algorithms.
\end{enumerate}

Therefore, investigating lightweight encoders within effective architectures like AS-Net is crucial for translating advanced research into practical clinical tools.

\subsection{Examples: MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2}

This study focuses on two prominent families of lightweight architectures:

\textbf{MobileNetV3} \parencite{howard2019searching} represents the third iteration of Google's MobileNet series, designed for high efficiency on mobile CPUs. It utilizes key building blocks like inverted residual structures with linear bottlenecks and depthwise separable convolutions to drastically reduce parameter count and computation. MobileNetV3 further incorporates Squeeze-and-Excitation modules for channel attention and uses neural architecture search (NAS) to optimize the network structure, including activation functions (like h-swish), achieving improved accuracy-latency trade-offs compared to its predecessors. It comes in 'Large' and 'Small' variants, offering different points on this trade-off curve.

\textbf{EfficientNetV2} \parencite{tan2021efficientnetv2} is the successor to the highly successful EfficientNet family. While EfficientNet focused on optimizing accuracy under computational constraints using compound scaling, EfficientNetV2 aims to improve both training speed and parameter efficiency. It introduces new building blocks like Fused-MBConv (combining depthwise and 1x1 convolutions into a single regular convolution for efficiency, especially early in the network) and employs progressive training strategies (gradually increasing image size and regularization). EfficientNetV2 models (e.g., B0, B1, B2...) generally train faster and achieve better parameter efficiency than the original EfficientNets and other contemporary models. Studies like \textcite{lin2024brain} have shown the potential of using EfficientNet variants as encoders in U-Net like structures for medical tasks like lung segmentation, achieving good performance efficiently.

Both MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2 have been extensively studied and evaluated across various computer vision tasks, demonstrating their ability to achieve high accuracy while significantly reducing computational complexity and memory footprint. Their applicability to medical imaging is increasingly recognized; for instance, \textcite{pavithra2023mobilenetv2} demonstrated the use of MobileNetV2 as an encoder within a U-Net architecture for brain tumor segmentation, highlighting the feasibility of using such lightweight backbones for this specific task. However, their specific performance in the context of MRI-based brain tumor segmentation, particularly when integrated into the AS-Net architecture, has not been thoroughly investigated.

\section{Summary of Literature Review}

The literature review highlights the evolution of brain tumor segmentation from traditional methods, with their inherent limitations, to deep learning approaches, particularly CNNs like U-Net, which offer significantly improved accuracy. However, challenges related to data scarcity, tumor heterogeneity, and computational cost persist. Attention mechanisms have emerged as a promising technique to enhance deep learning models by enabling them to focus on relevant image features, with demonstrated benefits in medical segmentation. The AS-Net architecture specifically leverages a synergistic combination of spatial and channel attention, showing strong performance in skin lesion segmentation but suffering from computational inefficiency due to its VGG16 encoder. Concurrently, advancements in network design have produced lightweight architectures like MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2, which achieve high accuracy with reduced computational demands. This review establishes the context and rationale for the present study: to investigate whether integrating these modern lightweight encoders into the AS-Net framework can yield an accurate *and* computationally efficient solution for the critical task of MRI brain tumor segmentation, thereby addressing a key limitation of the original AS-Net and contributing to the development of more practical clinical tools.
