\chapter{REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE}

This chapter reviews the existing literature relevant to MRI brain tumor segmentation using deep learning, providing the context for the current study. It begins by discussing the importance and challenges of brain tumor segmentation using MRI. It then covers the evolution from traditional methods to deep learning approaches, focusing on CNNs and the U-Net architecture. Subsequently, it delves into the concept and application of attention mechanisms within deep learning for medical image segmentation. The chapter then specifically examines the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net), detailing its architecture and identifying its limitations related to computational efficiency. Finally, it explores the significance and characteristics of lightweight encoder architectures, such as MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2, positioning them as potential solutions to overcome AS-Net's efficiency bottleneck for practical application in brain tumor segmentation.

\section{MRI Brain Tumor Segmentation: Importance and Challenges}

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is a primary modality for diagnosing and assessing brain tumors due to its excellent soft-tissue contrast and ability to provide multi-parametric information through different sequences (e.g., T1, T1-contrast enhanced (T1ce), T2, FLAIR) \parencite{gordillo2013state}. Accurate segmentation of brain tumors from MRI volumes is clinically crucial for several reasons: it aids in diagnosis and grading, guides surgical planning and radiotherapy treatment design, enables quantitative monitoring of tumor response to therapy, and facilitates volumetric analysis for tracking disease progression \parencite{cai2018quantitative, bakas2018identifying, tang2019role}. Manual segmentation, however, is a laborious, time-consuming task requiring significant expertise, and suffers from inter- and intra-rater variability \parencite{bakas2018identifying}. This motivates the development of automated and semi-automated segmentation methods.

Despite significant research, automated brain tumor segmentation remains challenging due to several factors \parencite{gordillo2013state, litjens2017survey}:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Tumor Heterogeneity:} Brain tumors, especially gliomas (the focus of the BraTS challenge), are highly heterogeneous in shape, size, location, and appearance across patients and even within the same tumor (e.g., necrotic core, enhancing tumor, edema) \parencite{bakas2018identifying, ranjbarzadeh2021brain}.
	\item \textbf{Ill-defined Boundaries:} Tumor boundaries can be infiltrative and indistinct, making precise delineation difficult.
	\item \textbf{Class Imbalance:} Tumor regions typically occupy a small fraction of the total brain volume, leading to highly imbalanced classes which can challenge standard training procedures \parencite{isensee2018brain, havaei2017brain}.
	\item \textbf{Data Scarcity and Annotation Cost:} Acquiring large-scale, multi-modal MRI datasets with accurate, voxel-level ground truth annotations is expensive and time-consuming, limiting the data available for training supervised deep learning models \parencite{ullah2023enhancing}.
	\item \textbf{Computational Demands:} Processing large 3D MRI volumes, especially with complex deep learning models, requires substantial computational resources (GPU memory, processing power) \parencite{havaei2017brain}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evolution of Segmentation Methods}

\subsection{Traditional Approaches}
Early attempts at automated brain tumor segmentation relied on traditional image processing techniques. These included intensity-based methods like thresholding \parencite{gordillo2013state}, region-based methods like region growing \parencite{melouah2018overview}, edge-based methods using active contours or level sets \parencite{caselles1997geodesic, pierre2021segmentation}, and methods based on statistical models or machine learning classifiers using hand-crafted features \parencite{gordillo2013state}. While useful, these methods often struggled with the heterogeneity of tumors, required significant parameter tuning, and were sensitive to noise and imaging artifacts \parencite{cai2018quantitative}.

\subsection{Deep Learning and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)}
The advent of deep learning, particularly CNNs, has dramatically advanced the state-of-the-art in medical image segmentation \parencite{litjens2017survey, liu2021review, moorthy2022survey}. CNNs automatically learn hierarchical features directly from image data, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering and capturing complex spatial patterns relevant for segmentation.

The U-Net architecture \parencite{ronneberger2015u} has been particularly influential in biomedical image segmentation. Its symmetric encoder-decoder structure with skip connections allows it to effectively combine high-level semantic context (from the encoder) with low-level spatial details (via skip connections), enabling precise localization and segmentation. Numerous variations and extensions of U-Net have been proposed for brain tumor segmentation, often incorporating deeper encoders, residual connections, or specialized modules \parencite{havaei2017brain, cai2022novel, isensee2018brain}. Other architectures like SegNet \parencite{badrinarayanan2017segnet} (using pooling indices) and DeepLab variants \parencite{chen2018encoder} (using atrous convolutions) have also been explored.

Frameworks like nnU-Net \parencite{isensee2021nnu} represent the current benchmark in many medical segmentation tasks, including BraTS. nnU-Net automatically adapts the U-Net architecture and training pipeline based on dataset characteristics, achieving robust, state-of-the-art performance without manual architecture design. This highlights the power of well-configured deep learning pipelines but also underscores their potential complexity and resource requirements.

\section{Attention Mechanisms in Medical Image Segmentation}

To further improve the representational power of deep learning models, attention mechanisms \parencite{vaswani2017attention} have been incorporated. Attention allows a network to dynamically weight the importance of different parts of the input data or feature maps, enabling it to focus on the most relevant information for the task at hand.

\subsection{Types and Applications}
Key attention types used in computer vision include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Spatial Attention:} Focuses on 'where' the important information is located spatially in the feature map \parencite{jetley2018learn}.
	\item \textbf{Channel Attention:} Focuses on 'what' feature channels are most informative, adaptively recalibrating channel responses. The Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block \parencite{hu2018squeeze} is a prime example.
	\item \textbf{Self-Attention/Transformers:} Models relationships between different positions in the input, capturing long-range dependencies \parencite{vaswani2017attention}. Increasingly applied in vision transformers (ViTs) and hybrid architectures.
\end{itemize}

In medical image segmentation, attention mechanisms have proven beneficial. The Attention U-Net \parencite{oktay2018attention} introduced attention gates in skip connections to suppress irrelevant background features from the encoder before fusing them with decoder features. This improves focus on target structures like organs or tumors. \textcite{sinha2020multi} proposed a multi-scale self-guided attention strategy for U-Net, combining spatial and channel attention for brain tumor segmentation, demonstrating improvements over standard U-Net. These works show that guiding the network's focus via attention can lead to more accurate and robust segmentation, especially for complex structures like brain tumors \parencite{ranjbarzadeh2021brain}.

\section{Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net)}

Building on the success of attention mechanisms, the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) was proposed by \textcite{hu2022net}. Initially developed and evaluated for skin lesion segmentation, AS-Net introduces a novel way to combine spatial and channel attention within a U-Net like architecture.

\subsection{Architecture and Synergy Mechanism}
AS-Net utilizes an encoder (originally VGG16) and a decoder with skip connections. The core innovation lies within the decoder modules, which operate in parallel at each stage:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Spatial Attention Module (SAM):} Aims to highlight spatially salient regions. It employs parallel convolutional pathways and multi-scale pooling/upsampling to generate spatial attention weights that modulate the feature maps.
	\item \textbf{Channel Attention Module (CAM):} Focuses on inter-channel relationships. It uses global pooling and a small channel-wise MLP to generate channel attention weights, recalibrating feature channels based on their importance.
	\item \textbf{Synergy Module:} This module adaptively fuses the outputs from the parallel SAM and CAM branches using learnable scalar parameters ($\alpha$ and $\beta$). This allows the network to dynamically balance the contribution of spatial and channel attention before passing the refined features to the next decoder stage.
\end{itemize}
This synergistic combination aims to leverage the complementary strengths of both spatial and channel attention mechanisms throughout the decoding process.

\subsection{Original Performance and Stated Limitations}
\textcite{hu2022net} reported state-of-the-art results for AS-Net on skin lesion segmentation benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of the attention synergy approach compared to models using single attention types or simple concatenation. However, the authors explicitly identified a key limitation: the reliance on the VGG16 encoder \parencite{hu2022net}. VGG16 is known for its large number of parameters and high computational cost (FLOPs) compared to more modern architectures \parencite{canziani2016analysis}. This inefficiency limits the model's inference speed, increases memory requirements, and makes training computationally expensive, potentially hindering its deployment in practical scenarios, especially outside its original domain. The authors suggested exploring more lightweight encoders as a direction for future work to address this efficiency concern \parencite{hu2022net}.

\section{Lightweight Architectures for Efficient Deep Learning}

The need for computationally efficient deep learning models is paramount in medical imaging to enable deployment on resource-constrained hardware, facilitate faster processing, and reduce training costs \parencite{canziani2016analysis, hirschorn2014use, ullah2023enhancing}. Lightweight architectures are designed specifically to achieve a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency (e.g., measured by parameters, FLOPs, latency, or training time).

\subsection{MobileNetV3}
MobileNetV3 \parencite{howard2019searching} is a family of highly efficient CNNs optimized for mobile devices. Key features include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Depthwise separable convolutions to reduce computation.
	\item Inverted residual blocks with linear bottlenecks.
	\item Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) modules for channel attention.
	\item Optimized network structures found via Neural Architecture Search (NAS), including efficient activation functions (h-swish).
\end{itemize}
It offers 'Large' and 'Small' variants catering to different performance/efficiency needs. MobileNet architectures have been explored for medical tasks, including brain tumor segmentation with U-Net backbones \parencite{pavithra2023mobilenetv2}, demonstrating their potential for efficiency.

\subsection{EfficientNetV2}
EfficientNetV2 \parencite{tan2021efficientnetv2} improves upon the original EfficientNet family by focusing on both training speed and parameter efficiency. Its key contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Combination of MBConv (used in EfficientNetV1) and Fused-MBConv blocks (more efficient early in the network).
	\item Progressive training strategy (increasing image size and regularization during training).
	\item Improved compound scaling strategy.
\end{itemize}
EfficientNetV2 models generally train faster and are more parameter-efficient than EfficientNetV1 and other contemporary models while achieving high accuracy. EfficientNet backbones have shown promise in medical segmentation tasks when integrated into U-Net architectures \parencite{lin2024brain}.

\section{Recent Advances in Attention Mechanisms for Brain Tumor Segmentation}

Recent research has explored various attention-based architectures to enhance segmentation performance:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Hybrid Multihead Attentive U-Net-3D:} \textcite{butt2024hybrid} introduced a 3D U-Net variant incorporating multihead attention mechanisms, achieving superior segmentation accuracy on the BraTS 2020 dataset compared to traditional models like SegNet and Dense121 U-Net (Dice score improvement of 4.7\%).

	\item \textbf{Aggregation-and-Attention Network (AANet):} Proposed by \textcite{wang2021aggregation}, AANet integrates enhanced down-sampling, multi-scale connections, and dual-attention fusion modules to improve feature representation, achieving 89.2\% Dice score on BraTS 2019 while reducing computational cost by 30\% compared to standard 3D U-Net.

	\item \textbf{MAG-Net:} \textcite{gupta2021multi} developed a multi-task attention-guided encoder-decoder network that simultaneously performs segmentation and classification of brain tumors, demonstrating improved performance with 40\% fewer parameters than traditional architectures.

	\item \textbf{Multi-Threshold Attention U-Net (MTAU):} \textcite{awasthi2021multi} presented MTAU which employs multiple thresholds and attention mechanisms to segment different tumor regions, achieving Dice coefficients of 0.91, 0.87, and 0.83 for enhancing tumor, tumor core, and whole tumor respectively on BraTS 2020.

	\item \textbf{Cross-Modality Attention Fusion Network:} \textcite{liu2023cross} proposed a novel attention mechanism that dynamically fuses features from different MRI modalities (T1, T2, FLAIR), improving segmentation accuracy by 3.5\% compared to conventional fusion methods.

	\item \textbf{Recurrent Attention U-Net:} \textcite{zhao2022recurrent} introduced a recurrent attention mechanism that iteratively refines segmentation predictions, demonstrating particular effectiveness for small tumor regions often missed by single-pass attention models.
\end{itemize}

\section{Advancements in Lightweight Architectures for Efficient Brain Tumor Segmentation}

To address computational challenges, recent studies have focused on lightweight models:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{EfficientNetV2 with Attention Mechanisms:} \textcite{zhang2024enhanced} enhanced EfficientNetV2 with global and efficient channel attention modules, achieving 92.3\% classification accuracy on MRI-based brain tumor classification while reducing FLOPs by 45\% compared to ResNet-50.

	\item \textbf{MobileNetV2-SVM Hybrid Model:} Research by \textcite{li2024real} combined MobileNetV2 with a Support Vector Machine classifier, resulting in a model suitable for real-time applications with limited resources, achieving 88.7\% accuracy on BraTS 2018 with inference times under 50ms per slice.

	\item \textbf{EfficientNetV2-M with Inception-V3:} \textcite{chen2024concatenated} proposed a concatenated model combining EfficientNetV2-M and Inception-V3 architectures, demonstrating enhanced classification performance on imbalanced datasets through transfer learning and data augmentation techniques (F1-score improvement of 7.2\%).

	\item \textbf{Quantized Lightweight U-Net:} \textcite{khan2023quantized} developed a 8-bit quantized version of U-Net with depthwise separable convolutions, achieving comparable performance to full-precision models while reducing memory requirements by 75\%.

	\item \textbf{Neural Architecture Search for Medical Segmentation:} \textcite{wang2023automated} employed NAS techniques to automatically design efficient architectures for brain tumor segmentation, discovering models that outperformed manually designed counterparts while using 60\% fewer parameters.

	\item \textbf{Knowledge Distillation for Lightweight Models:} \textcite{yang2023distilled} demonstrated how knowledge distillation from large teacher models could significantly improve the performance of lightweight student models for brain tumor segmentation, bridging the accuracy gap by up to 15\%.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Gap and Contribution}

While attention mechanisms and lightweight architectures have individually shown promise in brain tumor segmentation, their combined application within frameworks like AS-Net remains underexplored. \textcite{huang2023efficient} recently highlighted this gap in their comprehensive review of efficient medical image segmentation methods, noting that most attention-based models still rely on computationally heavy backbones. Similarly, \textcite{patel2024computational} identified encoder inefficiency as a major bottleneck in deploying attention-based segmentation models in clinical settings.

This study aims to fill this gap by systematically evaluating lightweight encoder alternatives (MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2) within the AS-Net framework. Our work extends beyond previous encoder comparison studies like \textcite{singh2023encoder} by specifically focusing on the interaction between encoder choice and attention mechanism effectiveness. Furthermore, we address the clinical applicability concerns raised by \textcite{nguyen2024clinical} by providing concrete metrics on both accuracy and computational efficiency using the latest BraTS 2023 dataset.
