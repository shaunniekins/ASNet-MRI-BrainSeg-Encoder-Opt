\chapter{SUMMARY, CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION}

\section{Summary}
This research focused on optimizing the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) for MRI brain tumor segmentation by comparing the performance and computational efficiency of different encoder backbones. Motivated by the high computational cost of the original AS-Net's VGG16 encoder, this study explored integrating lightweight alternatives—MobileNetV3 (Large and Small) and EfficientNetV2 (B0 and B1)—alongside VGG16. The methodology involved adapting the AS-Net decoder (featuring SAM, CAM, and Synergy modules) for each encoder, training them on the preprocessed BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset (2D HDF5 slices from T1ce/FLAIR MRIs), and evaluating performance on a held-out test set. Key metrics included Dice Coefficient, IoU, and total training time on an NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU.

The main findings from the test set evaluations revealed that AS-Net-VGG16 yielded the highest Dice score (0.8873). However, AS-Net-EfficientNetV2-B0 achieved nearly identical accuracy (Dice 0.8853), demonstrating the competitiveness of lightweight architectures. All lightweight variants significantly reduced training time compared to VGG16 (by 18-23\%, equating to 4.2-5.5 hours), with MobileNetV3-Large being the fastest. EfficientNetV2-B0 offered the best balance between high accuracy and improved efficiency (~20\% faster training than VGG16). The study also confirmed the effectiveness of the AS-Net attention synergy mechanism across these diverse encoders for this task.

\section{Conclusion}
Based on the empirical results obtained from evaluating the AS-Net variants on the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) test set, this study draws several key conclusions regarding the optimal configuration of AS-Net for MRI brain tumor segmentation:

Firstly, while the AS-Net-VGG16 configuration provided the marginally highest segmentation accuracy (Dice 0.8873) in this specific setup, its significant computational cost limits its practical applicability.

Secondly, modern lightweight encoders, particularly EfficientNetV2-B0, enable AS-Net to achieve segmentation accuracy (Dice 0.8853) almost indistinguishable from the VGG16 baseline. The minimal accuracy difference (~0.2\%) underscores the viability of using these efficient architectures without substantial performance sacrifice for this task. This finding is significant as it directly addresses the efficiency limitations noted in the original AS-Net proposal \parencite{hu2022net}.

Thirdly, among the tested lightweight options, EfficientNetV2-B0 presents the most compelling performance-efficiency trade-off. It nearly matches the accuracy of VGG16 while offering a substantial reduction in training time (approx. 20\% faster). Although MobileNetV3-Large trained fastest (~23\% reduction vs VGG16), its accuracy was slightly lower than the EfficientNetV2 variants. This highlights that architecture choices optimized for efficiency, like EfficientNetV2, can perform competitively in complex medical imaging tasks when paired with effective mechanisms like AS-Net's attention synergy.

Furthermore, the successful adaptation and strong performance across diverse encoder architectures validate the robustness and effectiveness of AS-Net's core attention synergy mechanism (SAM, CAM, Synergy) for enhancing feature representation in MRI-based brain tumor segmentation. The results suggest that combining spatial and channel attention synergistically is beneficial regardless of the specific features extracted by the backbone.

Crucially, this research quantitatively demonstrates that replacing VGG16 with lightweight encoders yields substantial improvements in computational efficiency, specifically reducing training times by 18-23\% (4.2-5.5 hours) on consistent hardware. This reduction is practically significant for several reasons: it accelerates research and development cycles, makes periodic model retraining or fine-tuning more feasible in clinical settings, lowers the computational resource barrier for researchers and institutions, and contributes to more energy-efficient and sustainable AI development. These efficiency gains enhance the overall feasibility of deploying advanced attention-based models like AS-Net.

In summary, this thesis demonstrates that optimizing AS-Net with an appropriate lightweight encoder, specifically EfficientNetV2-B0, provides a powerful and practical alternative to the original VGG16 configuration for MRI brain tumor segmentation on the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset. This approach successfully balances near state-of-the-art accuracy with significantly improved computational efficiency, paving the way for more accessible and deployable deep learning tools for clinical brain tumor analysis.

\section{Recommendations}
Based on the study's findings and conclusions, the following recommendations are proposed for practical application and future research directions:

\textbf{For Practical Implementation:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Prioritize \textbf{AS-Net-EfficientNetV2-B0} when seeking an optimal balance between high accuracy (Dice 0.8853, nearly matching VGG16) and significantly reduced computational cost (~20\% faster training than VGG16). This aligns with findings from \textcite{tan2021efficientnetv2} who demonstrated EfficientNetV2's superior parameter efficiency in medical imaging tasks.
    
    \item Select \textbf{AS-Net-VGG16} (Dice 0.8873) only if maximizing accuracy is the absolute priority and computational resources are not a constraint. However, as noted by \textcite{canziani2016analysis}, VGG16's high FLOPs may limit scalability in production environments.
    
    \item Consider \textbf{AS-Net-MobileNetV3-Large} (Dice 0.8738) if fastest training time is paramount and a slight decrease in accuracy is acceptable (~23\% faster training than VGG16). \textcite{howard2019searching} showed MobileNetV3's particular effectiveness in resource-constrained scenarios.
    
    \item Avoid AS-Net-MobileNetV3-Small due to lower comparative accuracy, consistent with \textcite{pavithra2023mobilenetv2}'s findings that smaller variants may sacrifice too much accuracy for brain tumor tasks.
    
    \item Validate the chosen model thoroughly in the specific clinical workflow and target environment before deployment, considering the study's limitations (dataset, preprocessing, 2D approach). \textcite{nguyen2024clinical} emphasizes the importance of clinical validation for AI models in radiology.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{For Further Research:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generalizability Assessment:} Conduct extensive validation of AS-Net-VGG16 and AS-Net-EfficientNetV2-B0 on diverse, multi-center datasets (e.g., other BraTS years, different imaging protocols) to confirm robustness. \textcite{bakas2018identifying} highlights the importance of multi-institutional validation for brain tumor segmentation models.
    
    \item \textbf{Multi-Class Segmentation:} Adapt the framework (output layer, loss function) to segment tumor sub-regions (NCR/NET, ED, ET) for enhanced clinical utility. The hierarchical approach suggested by \textcite{isensee2018brain} could be particularly informative.
    
    \item \textbf{Benchmarking:} Compare the optimized AS-Net variants against current state-of-the-art models (e.g., nnU-Net, Transformer-based methods) using standardized BraTS evaluation protocols. The evaluation framework proposed by \textcite{menze2015multimodal} provides a robust methodology.
    
    \item \textbf{3D Architecture Development:} Explore implementing the AS-Net attention synergy concept within a fully 3D CNN architecture to leverage volumetric MRI data context. \textcite{myronenko20183d}'s work on 3D brain tumor segmentation could serve as inspiration.
    
    \item \textbf{Encoder Exploration:} Investigate newer lightweight encoders (e.g., larger EfficientNetV2 variants, ConvNeXt) within the AS-Net framework. Recent work by \textcite{liu2022convnet} suggests promising directions for modern ConvNet architectures.
    
    % \item \textbf{Optimization and Ablation:} Perform fine-grained hyperparameter tuning for each encoder and conduct ablation studies to isolate the contributions of SAM, CAM, and the Synergy module. The methodology used by \textcite{hu2022net} in their original AS-Net paper could be extended.
    
    % \item \textbf{Inference Speed Analysis:} Measure and report inference latency on relevant hardware (CPU/GPU) to assess real-time potential. \textcite{khan2023quantized}'s work on quantized models provides useful benchmarks for efficiency comparisons.
    
    \item \textbf{Preprocessing Investigation:} Study the impact of alternative MRI preprocessing techniques on the performance of the AS-Net models. The normalization strategies discussed by \textcite{isensee2021nnu} could be particularly relevant.
    
    % \item \textbf{Knowledge Distillation:} Explore distilling knowledge from AS-Net-VGG16 to lightweight variants to potentially bridge the small accuracy gap, following approaches demonstrated effective by \textcite{yang2023distilled}.
    
    % \item \textbf{Clinical Integration Studies:} Investigate practical deployment challenges in hospital settings, building on the framework proposed by \textcite{patel2024computational} for evaluating clinical applicability.
\end{enumerate}

These recommendations build upon both our empirical findings and established best practices in the field. As emphasized by \textcite{litjens2017survey}, the translation of deep learning models from research to clinical practice requires careful consideration of both technical performance and practical constraints. The suggested directions aim to advance AS-Net's development along both dimensions.
