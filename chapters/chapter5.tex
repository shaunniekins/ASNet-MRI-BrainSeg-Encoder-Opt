\chapter{SUMMARY, CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION}

\section{Overview}
This chapter synthesizes the research undertaken in this thesis, which focused on optimizing the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) for MRI brain tumor segmentation through a comparative study of different encoder architectures. It begins by summarizing the study's purpose, methodology, and key findings as presented in Chapter 4. Subsequently, it draws conclusions based on the empirical evidence gathered from comparing AS-Net implementations using VGG16, MobileNetV3-Large, MobileNetV3-Small, EfficientNetV2-B0, and EfficientNetV2-B1 encoders. Finally, it offers recommendations for practical application and outlines directions for future research in this domain.

\section{Summary}
The primary objective of this research was to enhance the AS-Net architecture for MRI brain tumor segmentation by evaluating the trade-offs between segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency when using different encoder backbones. Motivated by the computational cost associated with the VGG16 encoder used in the original AS-Net study \parencite{hu2022net}, this work investigated the integration of modern lightweight alternatives, namely MobileNetV3 (Large and Small) and EfficientNetV2 (B0 and B1), alongside the VGG16 baseline.

The methodology involved adapting the AS-Net decoder, which utilizes Spatial Attention (SAM), Channel Attention (CAM), and Synergy modules, to function with each of the five selected pre-trained encoders. The models were trained on the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset, which was preprocessed from its original NIfTI format into 2D HDF5 slices using a custom script. All models used a 224x224 input size, except for EfficientNetV2-B1 (240x240). Training utilized a combined Dice and Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy loss function designed to handle class imbalance, with fine-tuning applied to the later layers of the encoders. Evaluation was performed on a held-out test set using standard segmentation metrics (Dice Coefficient, IoU, Precision, Recall, Accuracy) and by comparing total training times.

The key findings presented in Chapter 4 demonstrated that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The VGG16 baseline achieved the highest segmentation accuracy (Dice 0.8873).
    \item Lightweight encoders, particularly EfficientNetV2-B0 (Dice 0.8853), achieved accuracy nearly identical to the VGG16 baseline (difference of ~0.2\%). EfficientNetV2-B1 (Dice 0.8815) and MobileNetV3-Large (Dice 0.8738) also performed well.
    \item All lightweight encoder variants offered significant computational efficiency gains over VGG16, with training times reduced by 4.3-5.4+ hours (approx. 18-23% reduction). MobileNetV3-Large was the fastest to train among all models.
    \item EfficientNetV2-B0 emerged as providing the best overall balance, achieving accuracy almost identical to VGG16 while offering substantial computational efficiency (trained approx. 4.7 hours faster than VGG16).
    \item The AS-Net attention mechanisms proved effective across different encoders, contributing to robust performance overall.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Based on the empirical results obtained in this study, the following conclusions are drawn:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{VGG16 Baseline Achieves Marginally Highest Accuracy in this Setup:} The VGG16 encoder provided the best segmentation accuracy (Dice 0.8873) in this specific experimental configuration using the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset and evaluated on the test set.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight Encoders Offer Significant Efficiency with Highly Competitive Accuracy:} While not surpassing VGG16 in accuracy on the test set, lightweight encoders like EfficientNetV2-B0 (Dice 0.8853) demonstrated that segmentation accuracy nearly identical to the baseline can be achieved with drastically reduced computational demands (training time reduced by 18-23%, translating to 4.3-5.4+ hours faster). This confirms that integrating these encoders is a viable strategy for improving AS-Net's practicality.
    \item \textbf{EfficientNetV2-B0 Offers the Best Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:} Among the tested lightweight encoders, EfficientNetV2-B0 presented the most favorable balance, delivering accuracy almost indistinguishable from the VGG16 baseline on the test set but with significantly better computational efficiency (~20% faster training). MobileNetV3-Large was the fastest but had lower accuracy than the EfficientNetV2 variants.
    \item \textbf{AS-Net Architecture Generalizes to Brain Tumor MRI:} The successful adaptation and competent performance of AS-Net across multiple diverse encoder architectures validate the effectiveness of its core attention synergy mechanism (SAM, CAM, Synergy modules) for enhancing feature representation in the context of MRI-based brain tumor segmentation, as demonstrated on the test set.
    \item \textbf{Computational Efficiency is Substantially Improved by Lightweight Encoders:} The study quantitatively verified substantial reductions in training time (18-23%) when using MobileNetV3 or EfficientNetV2 compared to VGG16, even when VGG16 used a larger batch size. This makes AS-Net implementations with these encoders more feasible for researchers with limited computational resources and potentially suitable for clinical scenarios requiring faster model development or inference.
\end{enumerate}

In summary, this thesis successfully demonstrates that while AS-Net with VGG16 yields the marginally highest accuracy in this setup when evaluated on the test set, optimizing it with an appropriate lightweight encoder like EfficientNetV2-B0 provides a compelling alternative, offering a strong balance between virtually identical accuracy and significantly improved computational efficiency for MRI brain tumor segmentation.

\section{Discussion}
The findings of this study contribute to the field by providing a direct comparison of various modern encoder backbones within a specific, attention-based segmentation architecture (AS-Net) applied to the important clinical task of brain tumor segmentation using the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset. The results highlight that while established models like VGG16 might still hold a slight edge in raw accuracy under certain conditions, the gap can be minimal (~0.2% Dice difference to ENv2-B0), and newer, lightweight architectures offer dramatic computational savings (18-23% reduction in training time). The strong performance of EfficientNetV2-B0 underscores the potential of architectures optimized for efficiency to perform competitively in complex medical imaging tasks when combined with appropriate mechanisms like attention. The effectiveness of the AS-Net modules across different feature extractors further supports the value of incorporating attention mechanisms to guide segmentation models.

While inference time (the time taken to make a prediction on new data) is often the primary concern for real-time clinical applications and is typically fast once a model is trained, reducing training time remains critically important for several reasons. Faster training cycles significantly accelerate the research and development process, enabling quicker iteration and experimentation with model architectures, hyperparameters, and data augmentation strategies. Furthermore, models often require periodic retraining as new data becomes available or fine-tuning for specific patient populations or scanner types. Reduced training time makes these necessary updates more practical and less resource-intensive. It also lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and institutions with limited computational resources, democratizing access to state-of-the-art techniques. Finally, shorter training durations contribute to lower energy consumption, promoting more sustainable AI development practices. Therefore, the substantial training time reductions (4.3-5.4+ hours) achieved with lightweight encoders represent a significant practical advantage beyond just the initial model development phase.

These results provide a foundation for developing practical, high-performance deep learning tools where balancing accuracy and resource consumption is crucial.

\section{Recommendations for Practice}
Based on the study's findings, for practical implementation of AS-Net for MRI brain tumor segmentation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item If maximizing segmentation accuracy is the absolute priority and computational resources are not a major constraint, \textbf{VGG16} remains the top choice based on these test set results (Dice 0.8873), although the gain over ENv2-B0 is marginal.
    \item If balancing near-identical accuracy with significantly improved computational efficiency is desired, \textbf{EfficientNetV2-B0} is the highly recommended encoder backbone. It offers accuracy almost indistinguishable from VGG16 on the test set (Dice 0.8853) but trains substantially faster (approx. 4.7 hours reduction, ~20% faster).
    \item \textbf{MobileNetV3-Large} offers the fastest training time (approx. 5.4 hours reduction vs VGG16, ~23% faster) but comes with a noticeable drop in accuracy compared to VGG16 and EfficientNetV2-B0 on the test set, making it suitable only if speed is paramount and slightly lower accuracy (Dice 0.8738) is acceptable.
    \item Avoid using MobileNetV3-Small due to its significantly lower accuracy (Dice 0.8570) compared to the other options based on the test set results.
\end{itemize}

\section{Suggestions for Further Research}
To build upon this work, the following avenues for future research are recommended:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extensive Validation:} Evaluate the top-performing models (AS-Net with VGG16, EfficientNetV2-B0) on larger, more diverse, multi-center datasets (including different scanners and protocols, and potentially other BraTS 2023 tasks or previous years' data) to rigorously assess their generalizability and robustness beyond the specific BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset used here.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Class Segmentation:} Adapt the framework for multi-class segmentation to differentiate between tumor sub-regions (NCR/NET, ED, ET). This requires modifying the output layer, loss function (e.g., multi-class Dice, focal loss), and evaluation metrics.
    \item \textbf{Comparison with Other State-of-the-Art Models:} Benchmark the optimized AS-Net models (VGG16 and ENv2-B0 variants) against other recent state-of-the-art brain tumor segmentation methods (e.g., Transformer-based models like TransUNet, Swin UNETR, or nnU-Net configurations) on common datasets like BraTS 2023.
    \item \textbf{Explore Newer Encoders:} Investigate the integration of even more recent or specialized encoder architectures (e.g., newer EfficientNet versions, ConvNeXt, MobileViT) as they become available.
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Optimization:} Conduct a more thorough hyperparameter search (e.g., learning rate, loss weights, optimizer parameters) specifically tailored for each encoder backbone to potentially further improve performance.
    \item \textbf{Inference Time Analysis:} Explicitly measure and compare the inference times of the different model variants, which is crucial for real-time clinical deployment scenarios.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This thesis successfully adapted and optimized the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) for MRI brain tumor segmentation by systematically comparing different encoder backbones on the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset. The findings demonstrate that while the VGG16 encoder yielded the marginally highest segmentation accuracy (Dice 0.8873) in this study when evaluated on the test set, utilizing lightweight encoders, particularly EfficientNetV2-B0, allows AS-Net to achieve accuracy nearly identical to the baseline (Dice 0.8853) while offering substantial improvements in computational efficiency (significantly reduced training time by 18-23%). This work provides valuable insights for developing practical and effective deep learning solutions for medical image analysis, highlighting the critical trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency and paving the way for more resource-conscious tools to aid in the diagnosis and management of brain tumors.
