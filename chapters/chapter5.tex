\chapter{SUMMARY, CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION}

\section{Overview}
This chapter synthesizes the research undertaken in this thesis, which focused on optimizing the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) for MRI brain tumor segmentation through a comparative study of different encoder architectures. It begins by summarizing the study's purpose, methodology, and key findings as presented in Chapter 4. Subsequently, it draws conclusions based on the empirical evidence gathered from comparing AS-Net implementations using VGG16, MobileNetV3-Large, MobileNetV3-Small, EfficientNetV2-B0, and EfficientNetV2-B1 encoders. Finally, it offers recommendations for practical application and outlines directions for future research in this domain.

\section{Summary}
The primary objective of this research was to enhance the AS-Net architecture for MRI brain tumor segmentation by evaluating the trade-offs between segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency when using different encoder backbones. Motivated by the computational cost associated with the VGG16 encoder used in the original AS-Net study \parencite{hu2022net}, this work investigated the integration of modern lightweight alternatives, namely MobileNetV3 (Large and Small) and EfficientNetV2 (B0 and B1), alongside the VGG16 baseline.

The methodology involved adapting the AS-Net decoder, which utilizes Spatial Attention (SAM), Channel Attention (CAM), and Synergy modules, to function with each of the five selected pre-trained encoders. The models were trained on the BraTS 2020 dataset (preprocessed H5 slices) using a combined Dice and Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy loss function designed to handle class imbalance, with fine-tuning applied to the later layers of the encoders. Evaluation was performed on a held-out validation set using standard segmentation metrics (Dice Coefficient, IoU, Precision, Recall, Accuracy) and by comparing total training times.

The key findings presented in Chapter 4 demonstrated that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Lightweight encoders, particularly MobileNetV3-Large (Dice 0.9296) and EfficientNetV2-B1 (Dice 0.9237), achieved segmentation accuracy comparable or superior to the heavier VGG16 baseline (Dice 0.9249). EfficientNetV2-B0 also performed strongly (Dice 0.9227).
    \item All lightweight encoder variants offered significant computational efficiency gains over VGG16, with training times reduced noticeably (e.g., MobileNetV3-Large trained about 16 minutes faster than VGG16 in these runs). MobileNetV3-Small was the fastest but had lower accuracy.
    \item MobileNetV3-Large emerged as providing the best overall balance, achieving the highest Dice/IoU scores while maintaining high computational efficiency (second fastest training time).
    \item The AS-Net attention mechanisms proved effective across different encoders, suggesting their applicability to MRI brain tumor segmentation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Based on the empirical results obtained in this study, the following conclusions are drawn:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lightweight Encoders Enable Efficient and Accurate AS-Net Segmentation:} This research confirms that integrating lightweight encoder architectures like MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2 into the AS-Net framework is a viable and advantageous strategy for MRI brain tumor segmentation. These modern encoders allow AS-Net to achieve high segmentation accuracy (Dice > 0.92) while drastically reducing the computational demands compared to using VGG16. This directly addresses the efficiency limitations noted in the original AS-Net work and enhances the model's practical applicability.
    \item \textbf{MobileNetV3-Large Offers the Best Performance-Efficiency Profile:} Among the tested encoders, MobileNetV3-Large demonstrated the most favorable combination of high segmentation accuracy (highest Dice and IoU) and excellent computational efficiency (near-fastest training time). It outperformed VGG16 in accuracy while being significantly faster, making it the most promising encoder choice identified in this study for this specific task and dataset.
    \item \textbf{AS-Net Architecture Generalizes to Brain Tumor MRI:} The successful adaptation and strong performance of AS-Net across multiple diverse encoder architectures validate the effectiveness of its core attention synergy mechanism (SAM, CAM, Synergy modules) for enhancing feature representation in the context of MRI-based brain tumor segmentation. The principles underlying AS-Net appear transferable from its original application in skin lesion analysis.
    \item \textbf{Computational Efficiency is Significantly Improved:} The study quantitatively verified substantial reductions in training time when using MobileNetV3 or EfficientNetV2 compared to VGG16. This makes AS-Net implementations with these encoders more feasible for researchers with limited computational resources and potentially suitable for clinical scenarios requiring faster model development or inference.
\end{enumerate}

In summary, this thesis successfully demonstrates that AS-Net, when optimized with an appropriate lightweight encoder like MobileNetV3-Large, constitutes a powerful and efficient architecture for MRI brain tumor segmentation, capable of achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with improved practicality over implementations relying on older, heavier backbones.

\section{Discussion}
The findings of this study contribute to the field by providing a direct comparison of various modern encoder backbones within a specific, attention-based segmentation architecture (AS-Net) applied to the important clinical task of brain tumor segmentation. The results highlight the rapid progress in convolutional neural network design, where newer, lightweight architectures can now match or exceed the accuracy of established, heavier models like VGG16, while offering significant computational savings. The success of MobileNetV3-Large, in particular, underscores the potential of architectures designed for mobile or resource-constrained environments to excel even in complex medical imaging tasks when combined with appropriate mechanisms like attention. The effectiveness of the AS-Net modules across different feature extractors further supports the value of incorporating attention mechanisms to guide segmentation models. These results provide a strong foundation for developing practical, high-performance deep learning tools for clinical use.

\section{Recommendations for Practice}
Based on the study's findings, for practical implementation of AS-Net for MRI brain tumor segmentation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Prioritize using \textbf{MobileNetV3-Large} as the encoder backbone. It offers the best demonstrated balance of high segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency.
    \item \textbf{EfficientNetV2-B0} or \textbf{EfficientNetV2-B1} are also strong alternatives, providing comparable accuracy to VGG16 with much better efficiency. The choice between them might depend on specific resource constraints or minor performance variations observed in further testing.
    \item Avoid using VGG16 unless computational resources are abundant and the marginal potential differences in specific metrics are deemed critical, as its efficiency disadvantage is substantial.
\end{itemize}

\section{Suggestions for Further Research}
To build upon this work, the following avenues for future research are recommended:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extensive Validation:} Evaluate the top-performing models (AS-Net with MobileNetV3-Large, EfficientNetV2-B0/B1) on larger, more diverse, multi-center datasets (including different scanners and protocols) to rigorously assess their generalizability and robustness beyond the BraTS 2020 dataset.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Class Segmentation:} Adapt the framework for multi-class segmentation to differentiate between tumor sub-regions (NCR/NET, ED, ET). This requires modifying the output layer, loss function (e.g., multi-class Dice, focal loss), and evaluation metrics.
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Optimization:} Conduct systematic hyperparameter tuning (e.g., using techniques like Bayesian optimization or grid search) for the best models, exploring learning rates, optimizer choices, loss weights, fine-tuning strategies, and attention module parameters to potentially further improve performance.
    \item \textbf{3D Segmentation Models:} Explore adapting the AS-Net attention principles to fully 3D convolutional architectures. While computationally more demanding, 3D models can better leverage volumetric context, potentially improving segmentation accuracy, especially at slice boundaries. Lightweight 3D encoders could be investigated.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Utility Assessment:} Collaborate with clinicians to evaluate the practical value of the segmentations produced by the optimized AS-Net models. Assess their impact on treatment planning, response assessment, or correlation with clinical outcomes.
    \item \textbf{Comparison with Other State-of-the-Art Models:} Benchmark the optimized AS-Net models against other recent state-of-the-art brain tumor segmentation methods (e.g., Transformer-based models like TransUNet, Swin UNETR) on common datasets.
    \item \textbf{Explore Newer Encoders:} Investigate the integration of even more recent or specialized encoder architectures as they become available.
    \item \textbf{Model Compression and Deployment:} For potential clinical deployment, explore model compression techniques (e.g., pruning, quantization) applied to the lightweight AS-Net variants to further optimize inference speed and resource usage on target hardware (including edge devices).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This thesis successfully adapted and optimized the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) for MRI brain tumor segmentation by systematically comparing different encoder backbones. The findings clearly demonstrate that utilizing lightweight encoders, particularly MobileNetV3-Large, allows AS-Net to achieve high segmentation accuracy comparable or superior to the VGG16 baseline, while offering substantial improvements in computational efficiency. This work provides valuable insights for developing practical and effective deep learning solutions for medical image analysis, paving the way for more efficient tools to aid in the diagnosis and management of brain tumors.
