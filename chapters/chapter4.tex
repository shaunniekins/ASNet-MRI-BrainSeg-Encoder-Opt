\chapter{RESULTS AND DISCUSSION}

\section{Overview}
This chapter presents the findings from the experimental evaluation of the Attention Synergy Network (AS-Net) adapted for MRI brain tumor segmentation using different encoder backbones. Following the methodology outlined in Chapter 3, five AS-Net variants were trained and evaluated: AS-Net-VGG16, AS-Net-MobileNetV3-Large, AS-Net-MobileNetV3-Small, AS-Net-EfficientNetV2-B0, and AS-Net-EfficientNetV2-B1. This chapter details the quantitative performance metrics achieved on the test set, discusses the computational efficiency observed during training, presents qualitative visual results, and analyzes the implications of these findings in relation to the study's objectives.

\section{Experimental Setup Summary}
To provide context for the results, the key experimental parameters are briefly reiterated:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset:} BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) Training Data, preprocessed from NIfTI to H5 slices. 80/10/10 train/validation/test split applied to the slices.
    \item \textbf{Input:} T1ce and FLAIR modalities mapped to 3 channels ([T1ce, FLAIR, T1ce]), Z-score normalized.
    \item \textbf{Target:} Binary segmentation mask (Tumor vs. Background).
    \item \textbf{Input Sizes:} VGG16, MNv3-L/S \& ENv2-B0 (224x224), ENv2-B1 (240x240).
    \item \textbf{Architecture:} AS-Net decoder (SAM, CAM, Synergy) with specified encoder backbone (fine-tuned).
    \item \textbf{Loss:} Combined Loss (0.5 * WBCE(weight=100) + 0.5 * DiceLoss).
    \item \textbf{Optimizer:} Adam (LR=1e-4).
    \item \textbf{Training:} Max 30 epochs, Batch Size 32/replica (VGG16) or 4/replica (Lightweight), Early Stopping (patience=15 on val\_loss), ReduceLROnPlateau (patience=5 on val\_loss), Best checkpoint saved based on val\_dice\_coef.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation:} Metrics (Dice, IoU, Accuracy, Precision, Recall) calculated on the test set using a 0.5 threshold. Training time recorded for efficiency comparison.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

The experimental results allow for a direct comparison of the different encoder backbones integrated within the AS-Net architecture for the specific task of MRI brain tumor segmentation on the preprocessed BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset.

\subsection{Comparison of Encoder Performance (Segmentation Accuracy)}
Analyzing the quantitative metrics in Table \ref{tab:quantitative_results_updated} reveals several key insights regarding segmentation accuracy on the test set:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Top Performers:} The VGG16 baseline achieved the highest Dice Coefficient (0.8873) and IoU (0.7974). Among the lightweight alternatives, EfficientNetV2-B0 performed exceptionally well (Dice 0.8853), achieving accuracy nearly identical to VGG16 (difference of only ~0.2\%). EfficientNetV2-B1 (Dice 0.8815) and MobileNetV3-Large (Dice 0.8738) followed.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight Models vs. VGG16:} In this set of experiments, the lightweight models did not surpass the VGG16 baseline in terms of the primary Dice/IoU metrics, but EfficientNetV2-B0 came extremely close. This confirms that while VGG16 provided marginally superior accuracy, the performance gap to the best lightweight option (ENv2-B0) is minimal. The lightweight models still achieved strong performance overall (Dice > 0.87 for MNv3-L, ENv2-B0, ENv2-B1).
    \item \textbf{MobileNetV3 Variants:} MobileNetV3-Large (Dice 0.8738) significantly outperformed MobileNetV3-Small (Dice 0.8570) by about 1.7% in Dice score, indicating the benefit of the larger model's capacity for this task.
    \item \textbf{EfficientNetV2 Variants:} EfficientNetV2-B0 slightly outperformed EfficientNetV2-B1 in Dice score (0.8853 vs 0.8815), despite B1 having a larger input size (240x240 vs 224x224). Both performed better than the MobileNetV3 variants and were the closest lightweight competitors to VGG16, with B0 being almost on par.
    \item \textbf{Precision vs. Recall:} VGG16 achieved the highest Precision (0.8334), indicating slightly fewer false positives. EfficientNetV2-B1 achieved the highest Recall (0.9550), suggesting it was best at identifying actual tumor pixels, though likely at the cost of slightly lower precision. VGG16 and ENv2-B0 showed very similar recall values (0.9487 vs 0.9451).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Comparison of Computational Efficiency}
The training times reported in Table \ref{tab:quantitative_results_updated} clearly demonstrate the efficiency advantages of the lightweight encoders:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{VGG16:} Required 23 hours, 39 minutes, and 23 seconds to train (using a batch size of 32).
    \item \textbf{Lightweight Encoders:} All lightweight variants trained significantly faster, even with a smaller batch size (4). MobileNetV3-Large was the fastest (18h 12m 48s), followed by EfficientNetV2-B0 (18h 56m 27s), MobileNetV3-Small (19h 15m 18s), and EfficientNetV2-B1 (19h 22m 34s).
    \item \textbf{Efficiency Gain:} Compared to VGG16, the lightweight models offered substantial training time reductions, ranging from approximately 4.3 hours (ENv2-B1, ~18% reduction) to over 5.4 hours (MNv3-Large, ~23% reduction). EfficientNetV2-B0 trained approximately 4.7 hours faster (~20% reduction). This significant difference reinforces the value proposition of using lightweight architectures, especially given the near-identical accuracy of EfficientNetV2-B0.
\end{itemize}
This substantial difference reinforces the value proposition of using lightweight architectures for medical image analysis, reducing the computational burden for research and potential deployment.

\subsection{Effectiveness of Attention Synergy (AS-Net)}
The fact that respectable Dice scores (>= 0.857) were achieved with all encoder backbones suggests that the core AS-Net decoder architecture, featuring the synergistic combination of spatial (SAM) and channel (CAM) attention, is robust and contributes positively to the segmentation task across different feature extractors. The attention mechanisms likely help the models focus on relevant tumor features, even if the overall accuracy varied with the encoder's capacity and characteristics. This supports the hypothesis that the principles of AS-Net are transferable from skin lesion segmentation to MRI brain tumor analysis.

\subsection{Relation to Thesis Objectives}
The results directly address the study's objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primary Objective (Optimize AS-Net with different encoders):} Achieved through implementation and comparative evaluation. While VGG16 yielded the marginally highest accuracy (Dice 0.8873), EfficientNetV2-B0 (Dice 0.8853) emerged as the best lightweight alternative, offering a strong balance between virtually identical accuracy and significantly improved efficiency (~20% faster training).
    \item \textbf{Secondary Objective (Evaluate attention mechanisms):} The consistent performance across diverse encoders supports the effectiveness of the SAM, CAM, and Synergy modules for MRI brain tumor segmentation within the AS-Net framework.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Objective (Assess performance, efficiency, generalizability):}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Performance:} Quantified using Dice, IoU, Precision, Recall, Accuracy (Table \ref{tab:quantitative_results_updated}). VGG16 showed the highest Dice/IoU, with ENv2-B0 extremely close behind.
            \item \textbf{Efficiency:} Quantified via training time, demonstrating significant advantages (4.3-5.4+ hours reduction, 18-23%) for MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetV2 variants over VGG16.
            \item \textbf{Generalizability (within dataset):} The test set results indicate good performance on unseen data from the same distribution (BraTS 2023-GLI Task 1). Broader generalizability requires further testing.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Secondary Objective (Provide recommendations):} Specific recommendations based on these updated findings are formulated in Chapter 5.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
The limitations acknowledged previously remain relevant:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset Scope:} Results are specific to the BraTS 2023-GLI (Task 1) dataset. Performance on other datasets (including other BraTS 2023 tasks or previous BraTS years) may differ.
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Tuning:} The study used a fixed set of hyperparameters (LR, loss weights, etc.). Fine-tuning these for each specific encoder might yield slightly different results or rankings.
    \item \textbf{Binary Segmentation Focus:} Evaluation focused on the binary tumor-vs-background task, not differentiating sub-regions (NCR/NET, ED, ET).
    \item \textbf{Implementation Details:} Choices like skip connection points and fine-tuning depth could influence outcomes. The specific layer names identified for skip connections, especially for MobileNetV3-Small, required careful verification against the model summary.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Dependence:} Training times are relative and depend on the specific hardware environment.
    \item \textbf{Preprocessing Impact:} The specific method used in the \texttt{preprocess\_nifti\_to\_h5} script to extract and potentially normalize/resample slices from the original 3D NIfTI volumes could influence the results. Different preprocessing choices might lead to different performance outcomes.
\end{itemize}
Furthermore, the sensitivity of deep learning experiments to factors like specific dataset splits, minor code variations, hardware state, and random initialization is acknowledged, emphasizing the need for careful reporting of experimental conditions. The overall trend of lightweight models being significantly more time-efficient remains consistent.
